* **Gross Profit & Gross Margin:** Total revenue minus the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), representing the profit remaining after direct production expenses. A healthy gross margin leaves adequate room to absorb operational expenses, advertising, and net profit.


* **Seller’s Discretionary Earnings (SDE) or EBITDA:** Net operating profit with owner's discretionary expenses, one-time costs, and interest added back. This metric measures the true cash flow generated for a working owner or investor.


* **Net Profit Margin:** The percentage of total revenue that remains as net profit after all expenses, taxes, and fees have been paid. It reveals the ultimate efficiency and bottom-line profitability of the business.


* **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** The total amount spent on sales and marketing divided by the number of new customers acquired. It tracks how efficiently a store attracts buyers.


* **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV):** The total gross profit a single customer generates across all of their purchases over the entire duration of their relationship with the brand. It helps determine sustainable acquisition spending.


* **LTV:CAC Ratio:** The comparison between customer lifetime value and acquisition cost, with a healthy benchmark sitting at 3:1 or higher. This ensures marketing investments yield strong long-term returns.


* **Average Order Value (AOV):** Total revenue divided by the total number of completed orders. Higher average order values make it significantly easier to absorb shipping and advertising costs.


* **Conversion Rate (CR):** The percentage of website visitors who successfully complete a purchase. It measures how effectively the storefront turns casual traffic into paying customers.


* **Traffic Mix (Organic vs. Paid):** The proportion of visitors arriving through unpaid search versus paid advertising. Organic traffic provides structural defensibility, whereas paid traffic relies on ongoing ad spend.


* **Cart Abandonment Rate:** The percentage of shoppers who add items to their online shopping cart but leave the site without completing checkout. Monitoring this highlights potential friction points in the purchasing funnel.


* **Return Rate & Refund Costs:** The percentage of purchased products returned by customers alongside the associated processing and restocking expenses. High return rates erode profit margins and disrupt inventory planning.


* **Inventory Turnover & Stockout History:** How efficiently inventory is sold and replaced, tracked alongside historical frequencies of running out of stock. Avoiding stockouts protects sales momentum and preserves organic search rankings.


* **Platform & Fulfillment Fees:** The channel-specific costs (such as Amazon FBA fulfillment, referral fees, and storage charges) deducted from gross revenue. Auditing these fees is critical since they often consume the majority of gross sales.
